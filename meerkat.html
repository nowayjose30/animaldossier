Name and Status

Common Name

Meerkat

Conservation Status

Least Concern The meerkat is not currently considered endangered. It is found naturally in southern Africa.

Physical Profile

Size

Meerkats are small mammals with long bodies and tails. They usually weigh around 1.5 to 2 pounds. They are usually 10-14 inches long (25-35 centimeters).

Identifying Markings

They have grayish-brown fur, dark patches around their eyes, and a long tail. Their dark eye patches help reduce the brightness of the sun.

Habitat & Geography

Habitats

Meerkats live in dry grasslands, savannas, deserts, and scrublands. They dig underground burrows for shelter.

Distribution

Most meerkat populations are localized in Southern Africa

Ecology & Behavior

Diet

Meerkats mainly eat insects, but they also eat spiders, scorpions, lizards, snakes, birds, and other small animals.

Behavior & Adaptations

Meerkats live in groups called mobs. One meerkat often stands up high as a lookout while the others search for food

Communication

Meerkats use different alarm calls to warn their group about predators. They also use body movements and scent markings to communicate

Life History Cycle

Baby meerkats are born in underground burrows. They grow with help from other members of the group and eventually become adults that can find food and help care for young.

Breeding Behavior

Meerkats are cooperative breeders, which means members of the group help care for babies. The dominant female usually has the most important role in producing young.

Predators

Meerkats can be hunted by snakes, eagles, jackals, lions, hyenas, and foxes. They use alarm calls and hide in burrows to avoid predators.
