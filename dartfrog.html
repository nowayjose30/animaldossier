Name and Status

Common Name

Dyeing Posion Dart Frog

Status

This species is listed as Least Concern. It is still affected by habitat loss and the illegal pet trade.

Scientific Classification

Scientific Name

Dendrobates tinctorius.

Taxonomy

Kingdom Animalia
Phylum Chordata
Class Amphibia
Order Anura
Family Dendrobatidae
Genus Dendrobates

Physical Profile

Size

Dyeing poison dart frogs are about 3 to 4.5 centimeters long. They are small frogs that weigh only a few grams.

Identifying Markings

They can have bright blue, black, yellow, or white markings. Their bright colors warn predators that they may be poisonous.

Habitat & Geography

Habitats

They live in warm, wet tropical forests. They usually stay on the ground near small streams and other wet places.

Distribution

The dyeing poison dart frog is native to Suriname in South America. It lives in a small area called the Sipaliwini Savannah and nearby forest.

Ecology & Behavior

Diet

They eat small insects and other tiny animals, including ants, beetles, flies, mites, spiders, and termites.

Behavior & Adaptations

They are active during the day and can be very territorial. Their bright colors warn predators to stay away, and their sticky toe pads help them move across wet surfaces.

Communication

Male frogs make soft calls to attract females. Frogs can also use sight and smell to understand what is happening around them.

Life History Cycle

The frog begins life as an egg, then hatches into a tadpole. The tadpole grows legs, loses its tail, and changes into an adult frog.

Breeding Behavior

The female lays eggs in a wet place, and the male helps care for them. After the eggs hatch, the male carries the tadpoles on his back to small pools of water.

Predators

Snakes, large spiders, and dragonfly larvae can eat these frogs or their young. Their bright colors and skin toxins help protect them from many predators.
