Name and Status

Common Name

White-Throated Ground Dove

Status

The White-Throated Ground Dove is listed as Near Threatened. This means it is not yet endangered but could become threatened if its numbers continue to decrease.

Scientific Classification

Scientific Name

Pampusana xanthonura

Taxonomy

Kingdom Animalia
Phylum Chordata
Class Aves
Order Columbiformes
Family Columbidae
Genus Pampusana

Physical Profile

Size

The White-Throated Ground Dove is a small to medium-sized dove. Males and females look different, with the female being smaller.

Identifying Markings

Males have a white head, neck, and breast with darker bronze and brown feathers. Females are smaller and mostly cinnamon-brown

Habitat & Geography

Habitats

They live in forests and other areas with plants where they can find food and shelter. They spend much of their time on or near the ground.

Distribution

The species is native to islands in the western Pacific, including parts of the Mariana Islands and Micronesia. It has disappeared from Guam as a regular breeding bird.

Distribution Map

Ecology & Behavior

Diet

They search on the ground for food, such as seeds, fruits, and other plant material.

Behavior & Adaptations

They spend a lot of time on the ground, which helps explain their name. Their brown and bronze feathers help them blend into the forest floor.

Communication

Like other doves, they use calls to communicate with other birds. Their calls can help birds find mates and stay connected.

Life History Cycle

The birds hatch from eggs and grow into young birds before becoming adults. Young birds eventually leave the nest and learn to find food on their own.

Breeding Behavior

They reproduce by laying eggs in nests. The parents care for their young until the young birds are ready to survive on their own.

Predators

On some Pacific islands, introduced brown tree snakes have been an important predator of native birds. These snakes have contributed to the decline of the White-Throated Ground Dove on Guam.
