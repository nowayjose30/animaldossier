Name and Status

Common Name

Brownbanded Bamboo Shark

Status

Near Threatened. This means it is not endangered yet, but it could become threatened if its numbers keep going down.

Scientific Classification

Scientific Name

Chiloscyllium punctatum.

Taxonomy

Kingdom Animalia
Phylum Chordata
Class Chondrichthyes
Order Orectolobiformes
Family Hemiscylliidae
Genus Chiloscyllium

Physical Profile

Size

Brownbanded bamboo sharks can grow to about 3 feet (1 meter) long. Young sharks have dark bands that become less noticeable as they grow.

Identifying Markings

Young sharks have brown bodies with darker brown bands. They have long, slender bodies and wide, lobed fins.

Habitat & Geography

Habitats

They live in shallow ocean areas near coral reefs and muddy sea floors. They often stay close to the bottom of the ocean.

Distribution

They are found in parts of the Indo-Pacific, including areas around Southeast Asia and northern Australia.

Ecology & Behavior

Diet

They eat small animals that live on the ocean floor, such as crabs, shrimp, worms, and small fish.

Behavior & Adaptations

They are mostly active at night and use sensitive structures near their mouths to find food. Their bodies and fins help them move along the ocean floor.

Communication

Scientists know less about communication in bamboo sharks than in many land animals. Sharks can use body movements and other senses to respond to their surroundings.

Life History Cycle

The female lays eggs on the ocean floor. The baby shark grows inside the egg by using the yolk for food and then hatches as a small, free-swimming shark.

Breeding Behavior

Bamboo sharks reproduce by laying eggs. The eggs are released in pairs, and the babies hatch after developing inside the egg case.

Predators

Larger sharks and some marine mammals may prey on bamboo sharks. Young sharks are especially vulnerable to larger predators.
